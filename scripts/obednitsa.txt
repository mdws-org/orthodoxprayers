A bell is tolled before the Service of the
Hours begins. Toward the end of the Hours,
the bells are rung joyously.
A small Gospelbook and a hand Cross are
kept on its own Analoy or both on a small
table on the Amvon in front of the Royal
Doors, and for services they are brought to
the foot of the Amvon or the Cross to the

[p. 10b]
middle of the Nave.
The one who is to read the Epistle enters
the Sanctuary during the singing of the Tri-
sagion, kneels, then proceeds around the
Prestol and exits out of the left (north) door
and to the middle of the Nave. There he in-
tones the prokeimenon and reads the Epistle
Lesson for the day.
After the "Alleluias," the Reader or an-
other approaches the Analoy with the Gospel
and reads the Gospel Lesson for the day. The
usual practice is to face the Altar while reading
the Gospel, standing near the foot of the
Amvon.
A practice that is common is for the senior
Reader to begin the services and also to take
the "Through the prayers of our holy Fa-
thers," etc.
At the end of the service, the people
venerate the Holy Cross. After the veneration
and closing of the Service, the Gospel and the
Cross are put back in their customary places.
